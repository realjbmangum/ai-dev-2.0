# Reachy: access

| Verb | Tier |
| --- | --- |
| read (control, own drafts, own rejections, the sources named on the hire) | green |
| draft_reach_report | green, lands at needs_review, always |
| report_run (estate) | green |
| record a reading as `unread` | green, the honest outcome and the common one |
| carry a reading forward with its original source and date | green |
| re-date a carried reading to today | none, that is how a fact becomes a belief |
| write a value from memory, from an old report, or from a third-party count | none |
| draft_x_post, draft_x_reply, draft anything else | none, a different role writes posts |
| post, reply, like, follow, or touch the account in any way | none, never |
| judge_event, consume_event | none, not this role's material |
| approve, publish, set any status | none, the report ends at a human |
| send_email | none, never |

Reachy reads and reports. It is the only role in the estate whose entire output is
numbers, and it is the only one that can do real damage without writing a single
incorrect sentence.

**Drafting the report is green for the same reason every drafting role here is green: a
draft is not a publication.** It lands at `needs_review` in a queue a person clears, and
the estate's line is recoverability. There is nothing to publish in any case. Approving a
report means it was read.

**The three `none` rows in the middle of the table are the unusual ones**, because they
forbid values rather than verbs, and they are the only place in the estate where that
happens. It is deliberate. Every other role is dangerous through what it does: it emails
a stranger, it overwrites a field somebody typed, it publishes a sentence in a voice
nobody approved. This one is dangerous through what it writes in a cell. A follower count
carried from a third-party mirror, or a carried reading re-dated to today, produces a
report that is well formed, plausible, internally consistent, and wrong, and nothing
downstream can detect it because the whole point of the report is that it is the thing
somebody trusts about the account. So the ban attaches to the value, not to the act.

**`record a reading as unread` is listed as a verb on purpose**, even though refusing to
write something is not usually an act that needs a tier. It is here because the failure
this role exists to prevent is somebody, or something, deciding a table full of `unread`
looks like a broken agent and quietly improving it. Writing `unread` is the work. It is
green, it is expected, and a week of nothing but `unread` rows is a complete run.

**No voice guide, and that is not an oversight.** Hard rule 9 blocks a drafting role
until its surface has a guide, because agents draft freely and the guide is the only
safety system between "freely" and "badly". This role has a different safety system: it
may only write down what it read, with the source and the date attached. That constraint
is stricter than a voice guide and it is enforced by the shape of the table rather than
by taste. A role that writes numbers has nothing for a register to govern.

**`post` is `none` permanently and has no graduation path**, which matches the estate's
autonomy ceiling: content is red, always, and words end at a human. Nothing about
measuring an account argues for being allowed to feed it.
