All five are implementations of the same three rules

One. Instructions are served, versioned and drift-checked, never embedded in the prompt. Two. Agents propose, humans dispose, and the gate is a status column not a convention. Three. A run that cannot say what it expected cannot be checked by anything.

The PRD's job is not to invent this. It is to name it once and make the other four adopt the pieces they are missing.

9. Questions the PRD has to answer

Is there one machine, or one machine per business?
Directory Machine is a generalisable engine that happens to be pointed at directories. Its role/roster/verb-tier model would work unchanged for Crown and Compass and jbmangum. Do we widen it into the estate engine, or keep three engines and only standardise the contracts between them?
Where does the registry live, and what regenerates it?
The live trigger list is truth for cloud routines, but it cannot see local tasks, worker crons or grok bots. Something has to hold all 26 and be regenerated, never hand-edited. That is the thing ROSTER.md rule 5 asked for and nobody built.
Do the local Mac tasks survive?
Four of them, two never proven to run, all dependent on the laptop being awake. Courier and Ship Notes exist only because a headless routine cannot reach wrangler or private repos. An HTTPS ingest endpoint would move both to the cloud rail. Is that the plan, or do we accept a Mac-dependent tier and just make it report?
What is the minimum a scheduled thing must do to be allowed to exist?
Proposal: it declares itself to the registry, it writes a run row with expected and actual, and it has a named human gate. Anything that cannot do all three does not get scheduled. That single rule kills most of what is currently broken.
One watcher, or none?
Tally did this and was deleted. Chiefy absorbed it and was deleted. Whatever replaces them has to be the thing that survives, which probably means it cannot be another routine that can quietly stop firing.
